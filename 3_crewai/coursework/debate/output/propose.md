Yes—porn sites should be banned.

The core reason is simple: porn platforms are not harmless entertainment; they are engines of exploitation, addiction, and social harm. They profit from turning intimacy into a commodity, often built on coercion, abuse, trafficking, and the dehumanization of performers. Even where consent exists, the industry normalizes the treatment of people as disposable objects for consumption.

Beyond exploitation, porn has serious public-health consequences. It is widely accessible to minors, shaping their understanding of sex through unrealistic, violent, and degrading content. That distorts relationships, harms expectations, and can contribute to compulsive use, sexual dysfunction, and emotional detachment. A society that accepts this as normal is accepting preventable harm.

Free speech is important, but it is not absolute when a market causes clear and predictable damage. We ban other products and platforms that exploit vulnerability or harm children. Porn sites deserve the same standard. Protecting dignity, children, and mental health is a stronger public interest than protecting a business model built on exploitation.

In short: banning porn sites is not censorship for its own sake—it is a necessary step to reduce abuse, protect minors, and promote healthier relationships and a more respectful society.
