I oppose banning porn sites.

A ban sounds decisive, but in practice it is the wrong tool for the problem. It does not eliminate demand; it drives it underground, where content is harder to monitor, performers are less protected, and illegal trafficking is even easier to hide. Regulation is more effective than prohibition because it allows age verification, consent enforcement, labor protections, and removal of abusive material.

A blanket ban also creates serious free-speech and privacy problems. Adults should be allowed to access legal sexual content, and governments should be very cautious about using censorship to police morality. Once you give the state power to ban lawful expression because some people dislike it, that power can expand beyond porn.

If the concern is minors, exploitation, or addiction, the answer is targeted harm reduction: stronger age-gating, stricter consent standards, mandatory verification of performer welfare, and tougher penalties for abuse and trafficking. That tackles the real harms without punishing consenting adults.

So the choice is not between “doing nothing” and “banning porn.” The better choice is to regulate it tightly, protect vulnerable people, and preserve adult freedom.
